Automotive and EV study in the UK and Ireland after Grade 12

2027 intake planning for a Pakistani CBSE student living in Kuwait. Built from the research report dated 11 September 2026.

Two very different markets

The UK has the far bigger vehicle manufacturing, motorsport, engineering and R&D base. Ireland has a smaller manufacturing sector, but several highly relevant applied programs in EV engineering, diagnostics, management and transport technology at much lower tuition.

The best fit is an applied or automotive-specific honours bachelor, not a short diploma. It should keep postgraduate study open while still teaching real vehicle systems, electronics, EVs and diagnostics.

A program counts as English taught only where the institution teaches it in English or publishes English entry requirements for it. 2027/28 data is used where published; otherwise the latest 2026/27 figures, clearly marked.

    The two systems in short

    United Kingdom. A major engineering, manufacturing and motorsport base, so more placements and graduate roles. The weakness is cost: international engineering tuition runs from about £17,000 to over £37,000 a year before living costs. Traditional BEng degrees can also be heavily mathematical, with less routine diagnostics than an automotive technology student expects.

    Ireland. A smaller manufacturing base, but moving fast into EVs, charging and service skills. The technological universities blend applied engineering, labs, management and work placement, and tuition is generally lower. The qualification level matters: an NFQ Level 8 honours bachelor gives much better postgraduate and post-study options than a Level 7 ordinary degree.

                    Country

                    Student visas, work during study and after

                    The two countries work quite differently, and the rules matter enough to shape which program he picks.

                    United Kingdom Student visa

                    The Student visa costs £558 from outside the UK, plus the Immigration Health Surcharge at the student rate of £776 a year, normally charged upfront for the whole visa length.

                    Maintenance funds must also be shown: £1,529 a month in London and £1,171 a month outside London, for up to nine months, on top of unpaid course fees.

                    Degree-level students can normally work up to 20 hours a week in term time and full time in official vacations, subject to the exact visa conditions. A placement year that is assessed as part of the course needs university and visa compliance.

                    Ireland Stamp 2

                    Eligible non-EEA students on Stamp 2 can generally work up to 20 hours a week in term time and 40 hours in designated vacation periods.

                    Visa-required students must show access to enough money. The published first-year benchmark is €10,000, though real living costs, especially in Dublin, can be higher.

                    The Third Level Graduate Programme depends on the award level. Level 8 graduates can get 12 months on Stamp 1G, Level 9 or above up to 24 months in two stages. Level 7 graduates are not included.

                    How long could he stay and work after graduating?

                    Bar scale: 0 to 24 months, the longest available on either route.

                    The 2027 change that matters most

                    Under the current UK rule, a non-doctoral graduate gets two years only if the Graduate visa application is made on or before 31 December 2026. Applications from 1 January 2027 onward get 18 months. A student starting a bachelor in 2027 should therefore plan around 18 months, not two years. Immigration rules can change again before 2030 or 2031, so recheck this closer to graduation.

                    UK and Ireland against four other options

                    The parent-level comparison

                    The UK is strongest when the family budget and his Mathematics and Physics preparation are both sufficient. Ireland is especially attractive for lower tuition, English teaching and an honours qualification with practical EV or automotive management content. Malaysia stays the lower-cost English alternative. The USA has the strongest applied automotive ecosystem, but usually costs more.

                    Application portfolio

                    Do not apply to 15 institutions. Use five to seven applications, split between core choices, value choices and academic safety options.

                    Timing checklist

                      Apply to TUS very early. For the 2026 intake, TUS closed applications from Pakistan after 30 April 2026 because of expected visa delays. That was a timing decision, not a permanent ban, but a 2027 applicant should apply as soon as the intake opens and get written confirmation that the application will be processed.

                      Four year roadmap

                      Build both sides at once

                      He should graduate able to explain and diagnose a modern EV system, understand vehicle electronics and data networks, and talk to customers and managers. If he later joins a family automotive business, that mix is worth more than narrow mechanical theory.

                      Build a project portfolio: diagnostic case studies, CAN-bus logging, EV battery analysis, CAD, vehicle testing or Formula Student work. Use every summer for industry exposure. Learn the commercial basics too: parts margins, warranty, workshop KPIs, labour productivity, customer retention, fleet contracts and EV charging economics.
